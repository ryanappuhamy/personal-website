Swiss power demand, forecast a day ahead

Tomorrow’s demand moves tomorrow’s power price, and the day-ahead auction closes at noon. The model forecasts Switzerland’s 24 hourly loads for the next day using only what a trader would know at 12:00, and is tested over 45 months it never saw.

01 / 05

Results

Every method is scored on the same clean hours. The model beats the naive forecast in every year, but the grid operator’s forecast is clearly better.

That is expected: Swissgrid has weather forecasts for the whole country, metered data, intraday updates and knowledge of large consumers.

Monthly error, out of sample

The model is retrained every month on the past only and scored on the next month: 45 months that cover every season.

Swissgrid appears only for January to August 2023, the one period where its published forecast is usable.

03 / 05

Checking the data

The first run said the model beat Swissgrid. Comparing every series month by month showed why: from September 2021 to December 2022 the published Swissgrid forecast is a copy of the actual load, and from September 2023 it is broken.

A few load hours are corrupted too: a value frozen for 10 hours, a December weekday at 40% of normal. Each hour is judged against its own normal level, and 62 of 52,608 hours (0.12%) are removed, each logged with its reason.

04 / 05

Inputs known at noon the day before

A forecast that quietly uses information from the future looks better than it is. The load used as input stops at 10:00 the day before, and the weather is the archived forecast made about 48 hours earlier, not the weather that actually happened.

Switching to weather forecasts barely moved the error: recent load already carries most of the weather signal.
