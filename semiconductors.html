Reading the semiconductor cycle from public data

Chip demand moves in multi-year booms and busts. The project builds an index of that cycle from free official data, tests whether it improves forecasts of industry sales, and whether an investor could trade it.

01 / 06

The cycle index

A dynamic factor model extracts what six indicators have in common: chip sales in four regions, Taiwan chipmakers’ monthly revenue and semiconductor stocks. It tracks industry sales growth with a correlation of 0.93 since 1987.

Peaks and troughs are dated with the Bry–Boschan rules used for business cycles. Each upswing splits into Early and Mid Cycle at its half-way point, each downswing into Late Cycle and Downturn.

02 / 06

Forecast accuracy

Sales growth is forecast every month with only the data published by then. For the current month a simple autoregression is hard to beat, since most of it is already known.

Further ahead the cycle index adds information, most of all at turning points, where a forecast is worth the most.

03 / 06

Forecasts against actual sales

Three months ahead, the models follow the cycle closely from 2006 to 2025.

2026 is scored on its own: sales grew 144% in a year, far outside anything in the data, and every model misses it. Tree models miss it most, because they cannot forecast beyond the values they were trained on.

04 / 06

Checking the 2026 data

An earlier version cut the 2026 data as an error: monthly sales jumps of 20–26%, never seen in 40 years.

Checked against the industry association’s monthly releases, the numbers were right. The data is back in, and measuring growth in logs keeps one extreme year from rewriting forty years of cycle history.

05 / 06

Trading the cycle

The strategy holds more SOXX when the cycle signal is strong and less when it is weak, rebalanced monthly after costs. It trims the 2008 loss but recovers more slowly, and earns less than buy and hold.

Chip stocks look ahead and price the cycle before it shows up in sales. Overfitting checks over the 16 parameter sets tried (deflated Sharpe 1.00, PBO 14%) confirm the result is not luck.
