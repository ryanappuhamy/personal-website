Reading the semiconductor cycle from public data.

Chip demand moves in multi-year booms and busts. I built an index of that cycle from free official data, tested whether it improves forecasts of industry sales, and whether an investor could trade it.

The index

One number for the cycle, estimated rather than hand-weighted

A dynamic factor model extracts what six indicators have in common: chip billings in four regions, Taiwan chipmakers' monthly revenue and semiconductor stock momentum. It tracks industry sales growth with a correlation of 0.93 since 1987.

  • Peaks and troughs dated with the Bry–Boschan rules used for business cycles: 22 since 1987
  • Each upswing splits into Early and Mid Cycle at its half-way point, each downswing into Late Cycle and Downturn
  • Growth measured in logs, so one extreme year cannot rewrite forty years of history

Checking the data

The "corrupt" data was the biggest boom on record

An earlier version cut the 2026 data as an error: monthly sales jumps of 20–26%, never seen in 40 years. Checked against the industry association's monthly releases, the numbers were right.

  • WSTS's August 2026 figure, $159.7bn (3-month average), matches the SIA release exactly
  • The data is back in, and the 2026 boom is scored on its own instead of swamping 20 years of normal cycles
  • Every model misses the boom; tree models most, as they cannot forecast beyond the values they have seen
Strategy

Timing the cycle cuts risk late and adds it back late

Hold more SOXX when the cycle signal is strong, less when it is weak (0 to 1.25x), rebalanced monthly after 10 bps costs. Chip stocks look ahead and price the cycle before it shows up in sales, so the signal arrives late.
